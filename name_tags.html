Frequently Asked Questions
What does the Hebrew name tag maker print?

A name tag for every student on a class list: the Hebrew name, a transliteration and the English name, with an optional extra line such as the class, grade or school.

Can it transliterate Hebrew names automatically?

Yes. From a name typed with nikkud it writes the transliteration in a Modern or an Ashkenazi style (Dovid, Avrohom). You can also type a student's transliteration yourself, which always wins, or leave it off.

Which paper and label sheets does it print on?

Plain Letter or A4 paper with cut lines, 1 to 10 tags a page; Avery label sheets 5165, 5126, 5168, 5164, 5395 and 5163; or desk tent cards folded in half, one or two to a page.

Can I bring in my class list?

Yes. Open a CSV file or paste rows from a spreadsheet: the columns are found by their English or Hebrew headers. A class made in the Hebrew Classroom Dashboard is already here: the two tools share their class lists.

Is the Hebrew name tag maker free?

Yes. It is free to use under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for non-commercial educational purposes. No account is needed: class lists are saved on your device, and an optional free account keeps them on your other devices too.

How To Use
  1. Type each student's Hebrew and English names, or bring them in from a CSV file. A class from the Classroom Dashboard is already here.
  2. Pick an automatic Modern or Ashkenazi transliteration, or type your own for any student.
  3. Pick a Hebrew font, bubble letters, a size and color for each line, a border and a background.
  4. Choose plain paper and how many tags a page, an Avery label sheet, or desk tent cards.
  5. Press Print and print at 100 percent, then cut out the tags or peel the labels.
