Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Holiday Guide?

Free Jewish holiday coloring pages and a picture guide to the holidays. Every holiday has its own symbol, a short description and when it falls, and six symbols drawn to color and named in Hebrew and English.

Which holidays have coloring pages?

All seventeen: Shabbat, Havdalah, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah, Chanukah, Tu BiShvat, Purim, Pesach, Yom HaShoah, Yom HaZikaron, Yom HaAtzmaut, Lag BaOmer, Yom Yerushalayim, Shavuot and Tisha B’Av. Each has a full sheet of six symbols and a page of its own.

Which holiday does the page show first?

The next one. The page opens with the next holiday on the calendar and how many days are left until the evening it begins, and Make its coloring sheet opens its sheet. Shabbat and Havdalah come every week, so they are not counted down to.

How do I print a holiday coloring sheet?

Choose the holiday, tick the symbols you want and press Print. You can put one, two, four or six symbols on a page, label them in Hebrew, in transliteration and in English, make the pictures and the words bigger or smaller, show or hide the Hebrew vowels, add a line for the child’s name and a dashed dividing line between the rows, and print on Letter or A4 paper.

Are the Hebrew words written with vowels?

Yes. Every Hebrew word on a sheet is printed with nikkud, the Hebrew vowel marks, so that new readers can sound it out. Turn the vowels off to print the words as they are usually written.

Can I print the pictures already colored?

Yes. Choose Colored in and the same sheet prints in color, for a poster or a matching game.

Is the Holiday Guide free?

Yes. Free for non-commercial educational use under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, with nothing to sign up for.

How To Use
  1. The page opens on the next holiday, with the days left until it begins.
  2. Choose Make its coloring sheet on any holiday: its sheet opens. Tick the symbols you want.
  3. Choose how many symbols go on a page, the labels and what else is printed.
  4. Press Print, or read about every holiday and its symbols in the guide below.
