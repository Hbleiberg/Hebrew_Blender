# Reading assignments in the Torah Trainer — a teacher's guide

Students read a Torah portion you choose, record themselves in the Torah Trainer, and send you the recording.
You listen, write a comment, and can ask for another try. Nothing is graded by a machine, nothing is public,
and a student needs no email address.

Everything happens in the Torah Trainer: **`ivritsuite.com/torah_trainer.html`**. Give students the same address.

## 1. Sign in as yourself

Open the Torah Trainer and sign in with your usual IvritSuite account (the account button at the top: Google, or
an emailed code). Open **Settings** (the gear) and pick the **Teacher** tab: *Teacher options* holds the two
buttons you will use, **Assign this reading…** and **Students, assignments and recordings**. (Signed out, the tab
only asks you to sign in; students never see it do anything.)

## 2. Make your students' logins (once per class)

1. Press **Students, assignments and recordings**. The page that opens is yours alone.
2. Press **Add class…** at the top and name the class. The popup stays open on the new class: everything about
   its students happens in there, and **Edit class…** next to a class brings it back. (Students you never put in
   a class live under *Students without a class*, with the same popup.)
3. Under *Add students*, type one name per line — a first name or a nickname is enough; it is what the student
   sees on screen. Type a last name too if you want usernames like `ruth.cohen`. If you keep a class list in the
   Classroom Dashboard, Name Tags or Flash Cards on this computer, the drop-down beside the box can add those
   names for you.
4. Choose a **Username style** (first name + number, first.last, initial + last name…) and press **Preview
   usernames and passwords**. Each student gets a username and a three-word password (like `blue-tiger-42`).
   Change any of them if you like — usernames are lowercase letters, digits and dots; passwords at least 8
   characters.
5. Press **Create … accounts**. The new logins appear in a table, and **Print login cards** prints one small
   card per student with their username and password and where to go. **This is the only time a password is
   shown.** Print or write them down, then press **Hide the passwords**.

Lost a password? In the class popup's list press **Reset password** next to the name: a new one is made and
shown once. For a whole class, **Set one password for everyone shown…** gives every student in the popup the
same password (handy for a first lesson), and **New password for each shown** gives each a fresh random one;
both show the new logins once, with *Print login cards*. **Remove** deletes the login and every recording it
made; the *Class* drop-down on a row moves a student to another class.

## 3. Assign a reading

1. Go back to the Torah Trainer (the button at the top of the page) and choose the reading as you always do:
   a parsha, an aliyah, or a verse range with the custom picker. Only Torah readings can be assigned (not a
   haftarah or a megillah).
2. Set up the reading the way you want your students to see it — vowel colors on or off and which scheme,
   transliteration, translation, the font and sizes, the layout — exactly as you would for yourself.
3. Open Settings → **Teacher** → **Assign this reading…**. Give it a title, keep the suggested code word or press
   *New code word*, set the maximum length (10 minutes to start), an optional due date, and whether
   **classmates may listen** to each other's recording (they never see your feedback).
4. **Who**: *Anyone of my students who types the code word*, one **class** (every student in it right now), or
   **Chosen students…** (tick them). Students you choose see the assignment in their Torah Trainer at once,
   with nothing to type; the code word still works for anyone else of yours.
5. **Verses**: every verse of the reading is ticked. Untick the ones to leave out (the student still sees them
   on the page, dimmed and marked *not part of the assignment*). If you had ticked verses in the Copy bar
   first, the list starts from that.
6. The box **Students see this reading with my display settings as they are now** is ticked whenever your look
   differs from the defaults; the line under it says what will travel. Untick it if students should keep their
   own settings.
7. Press **Create assignment**. The code word appears large — write it on the board or send it home — and
   **Copy link** copies an address you can post in Google Classroom or anywhere else: a student who opens it
   signs in with their username and the assignment opens by itself.

Each assignment has its own code word, and the same code word cannot be reused by you while the first one
exists. A code word only works for *your* students.

## 4. What a student does

1. Opens the same address (or your link) and presses the account button, then **Student? Sign in with your
   username**, and types the username and password from their card.
2. A panel titled *Reading assignments* appears above the reading. It leads with **You have N assignments**
   and an **Open the reading** button for each one you gave them; a code word is needed only for an
   assignment you did not give them directly (*Have a code word from your teacher?* below). With your link,
   the assignment opens by itself after the sign-in. The portion loads with every tool they already know (the
   chanted recording to listen to first, and so on). If you sent your display settings with it, a note says so
   and offers *Keep these settings* or *Use my settings*; nothing of theirs changes unless they press Keep.
   **Hide** folds the panel away to a *Reading assignments* button at the top of the page, which brings it back;
   a recording keeps running while it is hidden, and the button shows the time.
3. Presses **Record**, reads, presses **Stop**. The line above the buttons says how long they have recorded,
   and recording stops by itself at your maximum.
4. Presses **Play it back** to hear it. Not happy? **Record again**. Happy? Types a name for the recording
   (it starts as "Ruth's recording") and presses **Submit**. The line shows the upload's progress and then
   *Submitted!*
5. *My recordings* under the box lists every assignment they have joined, with your feedback once you leave
   some. When you ask for a re-recording, a **Re-record** button appears there.

Students should sign out on a shared computer (the account button → Sign out).

## 5. Listen and give feedback

The **Class view** on the assignments page is one table: every student against every open assignment, with the
status in each cell (assigned, submitted, re-record, approved). **Add** in an empty cell puts that student on
that assignment; **Assign a new reading…** on a row opens the Torah Trainer with that student already chosen, so
different students can get different readings.

On the assignments page, press **Recordings** on an assignment. For each student you see the latest recording
with a **Play** button, a box for your comment, and **Ask for a re-recording**. Press **Save feedback**; the
student sees the comment the next time they open the Trainer. **Approve** marks a recording done. The filter
at the top shows only those waiting for feedback, only those you asked to re-record, or every version a student
ever submitted (older takes are kept). The page also lists who has not submitted yet when the assignment has a
class, or whoever joined by code otherwise.

**Download all (.zip)** saves every recording of the assignment to your computer, with a small spreadsheet of
names, titles, dates, statuses and your comments. **Delete recording** removes one for good.

## 6. Closing, and how long recordings are kept

**Close** an assignment when it is finished: students can no longer submit, and its recordings are deleted
automatically **30 days later** (you can set 7 to 180 days per assignment under *Edit*). The list shows the
date. Download them first if you want to keep them. **Reopen** cancels the countdown. Recordings are in any
case deleted about 400 days after they were uploaded, whatever happens.

You can delete an assignment outright at any time; its recordings and comments go with it.

## 7. Privacy, in short

- A student login holds the name you typed, a username, a password, and the recordings and feedback for your
  assignments — nothing else. No email, no birthdate.
- A recording can be heard by the student who made it, by you, and by classmates only when you switched that
  on for that assignment. Feedback is between you and the student.
- Recordings are stored with Cloudflare (a storage provider) behind a private address that needs a sign-in;
  everything else is in the site's Supabase database. The full statement is in the
  [Privacy Policy](https://ivritsuite.com/privacy.html), section 7.
- Removing a student, deleting a recording, closing an assignment or deleting your own account deletes the
  recordings concerned.

## If something is odd

- *No assignment matches that code word*: check the spelling, that the assignment is still open, and that the
  student's login was made by you (another teacher's code words do not work).
- *Too many tries*: ten wrong code words in ten minutes lock that student for the rest of the ten minutes.
- *The microphone is blocked*: the browser asked once and was told no; the address bar's padlock or camera
  icon lets the student allow it again.
- Nothing records at all on a very old browser or iPad: recording needs a current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or
  Safari (iPadOS 14.3 or newer).
- An upload fails: the student presses **Try the upload again**; the recording is still there until they
  leave the page.
